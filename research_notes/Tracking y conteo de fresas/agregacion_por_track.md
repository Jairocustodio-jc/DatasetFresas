# Agregación por track: fusión temporal / multi-vista de clasificaciones por frame en una decisión por fruto

> Scope note for the report writer. Context: strawberry monitoring robot; each fruit seen in several frames from slightly different viewpoints; per-frame class comes from a single-stage YOLO (5 ripeness classes, per-box class probabilities) or from a two-stage pipeline (detector + crop classifier, softmax over 5 ordered classes). Goal: a defensible track-level fusion rule plus evidence that fusion improves accuracy / count bias under partial occlusion.

## KQ1. Which fusion rule performs best, especially when per-frame errors are correlated (same fruit, similar viewpoints)? Is there a vote-vs-probability-averaging comparison on video object classification with numbers?

### Takeaway
The classical result (Kittler et al. 1998) and the video-detection literature (Seq-NMS avg > max rescoring; +6.5 mAP over single-frame NMS) both favour **averaging per-frame class probabilities (sum rule)** over hard majority vote, max-selection or the product/naive-Bayes rule, because the sum rule is the most resilient to estimation errors; the product/log-sum rule assumes conditional independence that correlated viewpoints violate, and theory on test-time augmentation shows the gain from averaging shrinks to zero as views become perfectly correlated. No paper was found that directly benchmarks vote vs mean-probability on fruit tracks; the closest direct comparison is Seq-NMS (avg 51.4 % vs max 50.5 % mAP) and the set-classifier of Hwang et al. (CVPR 2022) that beats per-frame voting by +4.1 TrackAP50 on TAO.

### Cited Findings

**Classical fusion theory [INDIRECT]**
- Kittler, Hatef, Duin & Matas, "On Combining Classifiers", IEEE TPAMI 20(3), 1998, derive product, sum (average), min, max, median and majority-vote rules from one Bayesian framework; in their experiments the sum rule outperformed the other schemes, and their sensitivity analysis concludes the sum rule is the most resilient to estimation errors (primary excerpt) — [Kittler et al. 1998 (course copy)](https://people.cs.pitt.edu/~chang/231/y14/combineclassifier.pdf); same conclusion in the [CTU DSpace record](https://dspace.cvut.cz/handle/10467/9443).
- A review attributes the sum rule's advantage over the product rule to the sum "diluting" a single poor estimate, whereas a product is destroyed by one near-zero factor (secondary) — [arXiv 1502.02191](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.02191).
- Kittler & Alkoot, "Sum versus vote fusion in multiple classifier systems", IEEE TPAMI 25(1):110–115, 2003: analytically, for two classes with equal-strength experts and i.i.d. conditionally independent estimation errors, **sum beats majority vote under Gaussian errors**; for **heavy-tailed error distributions, simulation shows vote may beat sum** (abstract via mirror) — [Kittler & Alkoot 2003 abstract mirror](https://search.isc.ac/Inventory/10/95133.htm); bibliographic record on [Wikipedia (Josef Kittler)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Kittler). A 2004 follow-up reports that sum is not affected by unequal classifier strength while vote degrades when a weaker classifier is added — [Journal of Systemics, Cybernetics and Informatics 2004](https://www.iiisci.org/Journal/PDV/sci/pdfs/P658547.pdf).
- A 2026 evidential-fusion paper states that the minimum operator reflects a cautious / redundant-view attitude, the product operator corresponds to conjunctive fusion under an independent-source assumption, and majority voting ignores the strength of evidential support; across its protocols **no single fixed fusion rule dominated**, and views are "usually neither fully redundant nor fully independent" — [arXiv 2605.17038](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17038).
- Late-fusion literature distinguishes average fusion on predicted labels ("sometimes called majority voting") from performance-weighted averaging of view-specific predictions — [arXiv 2507.06026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06026).
- A mammography study found that the **same image-level predictions yield noticeably different patient-level AUCs depending on the rule** used (from full averaging to max-based selection) — [arXiv 2607.02394](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02394).
- DESlib documents ready-made average, product, majority-vote, min, max and median combiners (useful to reproduce a comparison) — [DESlib aggregation docs](https://deslib.readthedocs.io/en/v0.3/modules/util/aggregation.html).

**Correlated errors / diminishing gain from averaging [INDIRECT]**
- Test-time-augmentation theory decomposes the risk change from averaging into an accuracy term and a **diversity** term: the gain is strict only when the averaged predictions' errors are not perfectly correlated; highly correlated augmentations (or a model invariant to them) cancel the benefit — [arXiv 2602.00114](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00114); summary in [Emergent Mind TTA topic](https://api.emergentmind.com/topics/tta-model) (secondary).
- TTA gains are typically modest: no more than ~2 % top-1 on ImageNet (StableTTA preprint) — [arXiv 2604.04552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04552.pdf); horizontal flip alone ~+0.72 % in the Albumentations benchmark — [Albumentations TTA guide](https://albumentations.ai/docs/4-advanced-guides/test-time-augmentation/).
- Shanmugam, Blalock, Balakrishnan & Guttag, "Better Aggregation in Test-Time Augmentation", ICCV 2021 (pp. 1214–1223): simple averaging of 30 augmented views (2 flips × 5 crops × 3 scales) helps on average **but flips many correct predictions to incorrect**; they propose learned non-uniform weights, evaluated on ImageNet and Flowers-102 with ResNet-18/50, MobileNetV2, InceptionV3 — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/html/Shanmugam_Better_Aggregation_in_Test-Time_Augmentation_ICCV_2021_paper.html); [arXiv 2011.11156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11156).
- Albumentations recommends averaging post-softmax probabilities and notes that averaging in probability space gives less weight to over-confident wrong predictions than logit averaging would — [Albumentations TTA guide](https://albumentations.ai/docs/4-advanced-guides/test-time-augmentation/).

**Video object detection / classification: tubelet rescoring numbers [INDIRECT]**
- Seq-NMS (Han et al., arXiv 1602.08465): links boxes across frames into sequences and rescores each selected sequence with the **average** or **maximum** detection score. On ImageNet VID full validation (VGG): single-image NMS **44.9 % mAP**, Seq-NMS (max) **50.5 %**, Seq-NMS (avg) **51.4 %**, Seq-NMS (best-of-three combination) 52.2 %. Initial validation split: 44.4 / 50.1 / 51.5 / 53.6 % respectively (primary excerpt). Seq-NMS placed 3rd in ILSVRC2015 VID — [Seq-NMS arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.08465). → **Averaging beats max by ~0.9–1.4 mAP, and both beat per-frame by ~6 mAP.**
- Seq-NMS on stronger backbones: raises ResNet-101 single-frame from 79.2 % to 80.5 % mAP (SSVD paper), and FGFA+ResNet-101 to 78.4 % (FGFA paper); SELSA reports Seq-NMS has "only minor, sometimes negative" effect on SELSA (80.54 mAP) — [FGFA arXiv 1703.10025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.10025); [SSVD arXiv 2007.03560](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.03560); [SELSA review](https://liner.com/review/sequence-level-semantics-aggregation-for-video-object-detection) (secondary). → Diminishing returns of post-hoc temporal fusion once the per-frame model already aggregates temporal context.
- T-CNN (Kang et al., CVPR 2016 / arXiv 1604.04053): tubelet classification and re-scoring module computes statistics of detection scores along the tubelet (**mean, median, top-k**), feeds them to a linear classifier, then maps scores of positive tubelets to [0.5, 1] and negatives to [0, 0.5]; T-CNN won ILSVRC2015 with 73.8 % mAP on VID val (secondary summaries; a benchmark listing says 75.1 for the 2016 paper — conflicting) — [T-CNN CVPR 2016 PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2016/papers/Kang_Object_Detection_From_CVPR_2016_paper.pdf); [CUHK slides](https://webarchive.lib.cuhk.edu.hk/20210328154319mp_/http:/www.ee.cuhk.edu.hk/~xgwang/CUvideo.pdf); [survey arXiv 2004.00451](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.00451). A 2018 review puts the mAP gain from "direct use of temporal information" at **+2.9 to +5.6 points** across methods — [arXiv 1809.03193](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.03193).
- In the FGFA comparison, tubelet rescoring helped a single-frame baseline but gave **no improvement when combined with FGFA**, while Seq-NMS was the post-processing with the largest gain — [FGFA arXiv 1703.10025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.10025).
- Hwang, Heo, Oh & Kim, "Cannot See the Forest for the Trees: Aggregating Multiple Viewpoints to Better Classify Objects in Videos", CVPR 2022: a **set classifier** that labels a tracklet from all its viewpoints, replacing per-frame voting that the authors say is fragile to motion blur and occlusion; plug-in re-classification on QDTrack; on TAO, **19.9 % TrackAP50, +4.1 over QDTrack**, with gains also on YouTube-VIS — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/html/Hwang_Cannot_See_the_Forest_for_the_Trees_Aggregating_Multiple_Viewpoints_CVPR_2022_paper.html); [arXiv 2206.02116](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.02116).
- Video object detection work identifies occlusion, motion blur and defocus as the cause of per-frame feature deterioration and uses temporal aggregation from neighbouring frames to recover the correct label "in deteriorated frames in which a single image object detector would not be able to provide the correct object category" — [CiTIUS spatio-temporal CNN record](https://citius.gal/gl/research/publications/spatio-temporal-convolutional-neural-networks-for-video-object-detection/).
- Brookes & Burghardt (ICPR 2020, gorilla face ID in zoos): track-level **maximum voting 97.49 % mAP vs average voting 97.28 % mAP** — a case where max ≈ average (secondary slide deck) — [VAIB20 slides](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/rbf/VAIB20PAPERS/SLIDES/OBvaib20.pdf).
- Karpathy et al., CVPR 2014 (Sports-1M): spatio-temporal fusion networks improved only modestly over single-frame models (59.3–60.9 % single-frame vs 63.9 % best); slow fusion > early/late fusion (secondary lecture summary) — [Karpathy CVPR 2014](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2014/html/Karpathy_Large-scale_Video_Classification_2014_CVPR_paper.html); [Princeton course notes](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spring16/cos598F/42455.pdf).

**Confidence-weighted voting / weighting by detection score [INDIRECT]**

**Learned temporal models over track features [INDIRECT]**

### Inferences
- For a strawberry track the per-frame errors are strongly correlated (same fruit, same occluder, slowly changing viewpoint). Under correlated errors the independence assumption of the log-probability sum (naive Bayes / product rule) is violated and it will become over-confident: one confidently-wrong frame can veto the rest. The sum rule (mean of calibrated probabilities) is the theoretically and empirically safest default (Kittler 1998; Seq-NMS avg > max).
- Hard majority vote throws away confidence and is only competitive when errors are heavy-tailed (occasional wildly wrong frames) — Kittler & Alkoot 2003. For ripeness, confusions are mostly adjacent-class (see KQ-ordinal), i.e. not heavy-tailed, which again favours mean-probability over vote.
- Max-confidence selection (pick the single most confident frame) is the worst choice under correlated over-confidence; the Seq-NMS avg-vs-max gap and the TTA "averaging flips correct to incorrect" finding both show max/selection is dominated by averaging except when one view is decisively better (which is the argument for visibility weighting, KQ2).
- A defensible thesis rule: **confidence/visibility-weighted mean of temperature-scaled per-frame class probabilities, argmax (or ordinal expected-stage, see below) at track end**, with a hard vote only as an ablation baseline. Learned temporal models (LSTM/transformer) are justified only if the dataset has thousands of labelled tracks; the generic literature shows they are harder to train and not consistently better than simple pooling.

### Gaps
- No paper found that directly compares majority vote vs mean-probability vs log-sum on **fruit** tracks; the only quantitative vote-vs-average comparisons are generic (Seq-NMS, Kittler & Alkoot, Brookes & Burghardt).
- Full tables for Seq-NMS, T-CNN and Hwang et al. could not be fetched (arXiv/CVF blocked); numbers are from excerpts.
- No numbers found for Dempster–Shafer vs averaging on multi-view fruit images.

## KQ2. Evidence in agriculture that multi-frame / multi-view fusion improves ripeness/quality classification or reduces count bias, and specifically for partially occluded objects

### Takeaway
Agricultural evidence exists but is thin and mostly about stability and counting rather than ablated accuracy: an apple conveyor paper (2026) adds track-level aggregation explicitly to stop frame-wise defect-label oscillation caused by blur/occlusion/illumination; the avocado 6-view fusion model reached 90.18 % (+7.70 pts vs single-view VGG16, +7.77 vs EfficientNetB0); strawberry (Ogundele 2025) and blueberry (Wu 2025) pipelines use ByteTrack for per-class counting but excerpts do not reveal the per-track class rule. Evidence that averaging helps *specifically* for partially occluded objects is indirect (multi-camera person detection, occluded multi-view action recognition, video-detection papers naming occlusion as the failure mode that temporal aggregation fixes).

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The agricultural literature treats tracking mainly as a de-duplication device for counting; the only paper found that explicitly frames track aggregation as a *classification stabiliser* is the 2026 apple-conveyor paper. The thesis can therefore position per-track ripeness fusion for strawberries as a gap it fills, while citing the apple-conveyor paper and the avocado +7.7 pt result as the nearest agricultural precedents.
- The Häni/Roy/Isler "median of the 3 most-visible frames" rule is an agricultural precedent for **visibility-weighted or visibility-selected fusion** (weight by detected fruit pixels ≈ box area / mask area), directly transferable to weighting ripeness probabilities by box size or visible fraction.
- Under partial occlusion the visible colour distribution of a strawberry is biased (e.g., an occluded red tip makes the fruit look less ripe), so frames differ in *information*, not just noise; this supports down-weighting small/occluded boxes rather than plain averaging, consistent with Shanmugam et al.'s finding that uniform averaging can flip correct predictions.

### Gaps
- Exact aggregation rule, minimum track length and quantitative frame-vs-track metrics of the apple-conveyor paper (arXiv 2602.19278) could not be read; needs the PDF.
- How Ogundele 2025 and Wu 2025 assign one class per track (most-frequent? last? class-aware tracker per class?) not visible in excerpts.
- No agricultural paper found that measures classification accuracy as a function of occlusion level with and without fusion.
- No strawberry paper found that reports per-track ripeness voting or averaging.

## KQ3. Ordinal classes: does averaging the expected stage index beat argmax-of-mean-probabilities for adjacent-stage confusions?

### Takeaway
No source was found that directly compares expected-value decoding with argmax on adjacent-class accuracy for ripeness. The ordinal-deep-learning literature confirms that softmax ignores ordering, that the expected value of a nominal classifier is a recognised ordinal decoding option, and that ordinal errors should be evaluated with distance-aware metrics (QWK / MAE); recent maturity work shows annotator disagreement concentrates on adjacent stages and models maturity as a latent continuous variable, which supports fusing a continuous expected stage along the track.

### Cited Findings
- A standard softmax classifier ignores class ordering and can fail when magnitude, not direction, separates classes; using the **expected value of a nominal classifier** is listed as one ordinal option, alongside scalar-output-plus-thresholds methods — [arXiv 1812.07352](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.07352).
- Beckham & Pal, "Unimodal probability distributions for deep ordinal classification": predicted class can be obtained by argmax of the output distribution or a smoothed alternative; evaluation uses quadratic weighted kappa, which penalises distant misclassifications more heavily — [arXiv 1705.05278](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.05278).
- A squared-error reformulation on diabetic retinopathy data yields higher QWK than cross-entropy while still producing a discrete distribution; it treats classes as equidistant — [arXiv 1612.00775](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.00775).
- "To Each Metric Its Decoding" (2025) shows that the optimal post-hoc decision rule for a probabilistic (hierarchical) classifier depends on the evaluation metric and that argmax is not optimal for all of them (hierarchical, not ordinal; loose analogy) — [arXiv 2506.01552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01552).
- The `dlordinal` library implements ordinal binary-decomposition / ECOC wrappers for deep ordinal classification — [dlordinal docs](https://dlordinal.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_modules/dlordinal/wrappers/obd_ecoc.html).
- [AG] A 2025 paper frames fruit maturity as a **continuous probabilistic target** (mAP 85.6 % on a large fruit dataset) — [arXiv 2510.24885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24885v1); a 2026 follow-up on tomatoes observes **annotator disagreement concentrated near adjacent maturity stages** and models maturity as a latent continuous variable — [arXiv 2604.26084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26084).
- [AG] Ordinal quantification (estimating the distribution over ordered classes) is an active topic with regularisation-based methods — [arXiv 2310.09210](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09210).

### Inferences
- With 5 ordered ripeness stages and adjacent-stage confusions, averaging the per-frame **expected stage** E[s] = Σ_k k·p_k over the track and rounding is the ordinal analogue of the sum rule; it is the Bayes-optimal decoder for MAE/squared error, whereas argmax of the mean probability is Bayes-optimal for 0/1 accuracy. Since annotator noise itself concentrates on adjacent stages (arXiv 2604.26084), MAE/QWK is arguably the more honest thesis metric, which favours expected-stage fusion.
- Expected-stage fusion can be reported as a continuous "ripeness index" per fruit (useful for harvest-timing), while argmax-of-mean gives the categorical label needed for per-class counts; the thesis can report both and show the confusion matrix off-diagonal structure.
- Caution: expected-value decoding can produce a middle class when the per-frame distribution is bimodal (e.g., half frames "ripe", half "unripe" due to occlusion of the red tip); a unimodality check or a visibility weight mitigates this.

### Gaps
- No empirical comparison found (in agriculture or elsewhere) of expected-stage averaging vs argmax-of-mean on adjacent-class accuracy / MAE; this would have to be run on the thesis dataset.

## KQ4. How many views/frames before deciding (N-frame thresholds, diminishing returns), and handling of tracks that end early

### Takeaway
No fruit-counting paper surfaced with an explicit "count/decide after N frames" rule; in practice this lives in tracker hyper-parameters (ByteTrack `minimum_consecutive_frames`, default 1; `track_buffer`/max-disappearance N for retiring IDs) and in frame-selection heuristics (median of the 3 most visible frames). Multi-view 3D recognition shows strong diminishing returns after a handful of views (MVCNN: 12 views 89.9 %, 80 views no significant further gain; RotationNet: 3 views ≈ MVCNN with 80 views), and video detection shows post-hoc fusion gains shrink as the per-frame model gets stronger.

### Cited Findings
- ByteTrack (Roboflow Supervision implementation) exposes **`minimum_consecutive_frames`**: "number of consecutive frames that an object must be tracked before it is considered a 'valid' track", default 1; increasing it "prevents the creation of accidental tracks from false detection or double detection, but risks missing shorter tracks" — [Supervision trackers docs](https://supervision.roboflow.com/0.25.0/trackers/).
- ByteTrack association: two-stage matching, high-confidence boxes first, then low-confidence boxes to unmatched tracks by IoU — [Roboflow ByteTrack docs](https://trackers.roboflow.com/latest/trackers/bytetrack/); [Roboflow blog](https://blog.roboflow.com/what-is-bytetrack-computer-vision/).
- [AG] Grape-bunch counting robot: centroids matched by minimum distance between consecutive frames; unmatched detections get a new ID; an ID is deregistered after it cannot be matched within **N frames** (max-disappearance) — [arXiv 2412.10464](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.10464).
- [AG] Apple cluster counting: cluster tracked over many frames; count reported as **median of the 3 frames with the most detected apple pixels** — [arXiv 1810.09499](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.09499); CNN counting: mean of the 3 maximum predictions over corresponding patches — [arXiv 2208.11566](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.11566).
- [AG] Liu, Chen, Dcunha et al. (ICUAS 2017), "Robust Fruit Counting": Hungarian matching with Kalman-corrected KLT costs; "not every fruit will be tracked in all the frames they appear in"; SfM 3D positions reject double-counted tracks (close in 3D) and size outliers; the correction "can significantly improve the counting accuracy and robustness" — [arXiv 1804.00307](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00307).
- [AG] Matos et al., CVPRW 2024 (Vision4Ag), apple tracking under intermittent occlusions and low frame rates; method "reliable with both missing and false detections" (e.g., a leaf mistaken for fruit) — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/Vision4Ag/papers/Matos_Tracking_and_Counting_Apples_in_Orchards_Under_Intermittent_Occlusions_and_Low_Frame_Rates_CVPRW_2024_paper.pdf).
- [AG] MOrangeT (oranges): a relocalisation step for occluded fruit using 3D positions; fruit tracking differs from pedestrian MOT because fruits are visually similar and stationary — [arXiv 2312.16724](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.16724v1).
- [AG] 2025 stereo apple tracking benchmark (OC-SORT vs ByteTrack): total counting error 20 apples, RMSE 6.24 %, R² 0.96 — [hrcak record](https://hrcak.srce.hr/en/321662).
- [AG] Mango video counting captured at **10 fps** from a moving platform — [CQU record](https://acquire.cqu.edu.au/articles/journal_contribution/Mango_fruit_load_estimation_using_a_video_based_MangoYOLO-Kalman_filter-Hungarian_algorithm_method_/13453205).
- [INDIRECT] MVCNN (Su et al., ICCV 2015): ModelNet40 **12 views 89.9 %** after fine-tuning (88.6 % before); view pooling is channel-wise max over views; the 80-view setup "did not improve significantly over 12 views" (secondary) — [arXiv 1505.00880](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.00880); [liner review](https://liner.com/review/multiview-convolutional-neural-networks-for-3d-shape-recognition). RotationNet with **only 3 views** matched MVCNN with the full 80-view set — [arXiv 1603.06208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.06208). Reported MVCNN figures vary by source/setup (89.6 %, 91.5 %) — [emergentmind](https://emergentmind.com/papers/1505.00880); [arXiv 1906.01592](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.01592).
- [INDIRECT] Diminishing returns of temporal post-processing: Seq-NMS +6.5 mAP on a VGG single-frame baseline (44.9→51.4), only +1.3 on ResNet-101 (79.2→80.5), ≈0 or negative on SELSA — [Seq-NMS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.08465); [SSVD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.03560); [SELSA review](https://liner.com/review/sequence-level-semantics-aggregation-for-video-object-detection).
- [INDIRECT] Image-Pro tracking software reports counts of tracks discarded for falling below a minimum length — an example of min-length filtering as a standard track QC step — [Image-Pro IMcTracksInfo](https://help.mediacy.com/Image-Pro/Automation/11/html/T_MediaCy_IQL_Tracking_IMcTracksInfo.htm).

### Inferences
- The literature offers no canonical N; the defensible approach is to (a) treat `minimum_consecutive_frames` (ByteTrack) or an equivalent "confirmed after N hits" as the count threshold, (b) choose N by plotting counting error and per-track classification accuracy vs N on a validation set, and (c) report the curve (expect a knee at a few frames, by analogy with 3-view RotationNet ≈ 80-view MVCNN and 3-frame median rules in apple counting).
- Tracks that end early (fruit leaves the field of view, long occlusion, ID switch) should still be decided from whatever frames exist if N_min is met; the Häni/Roy "top-k most visible frames" rule is a principled way to decide from short tracks while discounting occluded frames. Tracks with fewer than N_min frames should be excluded from counts (false-positive suppression) and the number excluded reported, since the Supervision docs warn this trades recall for precision.
- Because post-hoc fusion gains shrink with detector strength (Seq-NMS trend), the thesis should expect modest accuracy gains from fusion when the per-frame YOLO is already strong, but large gains in **stability** (no flicker) and in **count bias** — which is what the apple-conveyor paper emphasises.

### Gaps
- No fruit-counting paper found that states an explicit minimum number of frames before a track is counted; thresholds appear only as tracker hyper-parameters.
- No ablation found of per-object classification accuracy vs number of aggregated frames for fruit.
- Matos 2024 and Dcunha 2017 methods sections could not be read (CVF/arXiv blocked).

## KQ5. Quantification: estimating class prevalence from noisy per-object labels (Forman 2008 ACC; González et al. 2017; PCC/PACC) and whether it is applied after tracking

### Takeaway
Quantification learning provides exactly the correction needed for biased per-class counts: Classify-and-Count is biased, Adjusted Classify-and-Count (Forman) corrects with the classifier's TPR/FPR estimated by cross-validation, and Probabilistic (Adjusted) Classify-and-Count uses the mean posterior instead of hard counts — which matches "mean of probabilities per track" naturally. No paper was found that applies quantification *after* tracking in agriculture; this is an open niche the thesis can occupy.

### Cited Findings
- Forman (2008), "Quantifying counts and costs via classification", Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery 17(2):164–206, introduces Adjusted Classify & Count: corrected prevalence **p̂ = (p̂_CC − FPR)/(TPR − FPR)**, clipped to [0,1], with TPR/FPR estimated by k-fold cross-validation on training data; Forman showed plain CC is biased (over- or under-estimates depending on prevalence) — [AEPIA quantification slides (Spanish)](https://www.aepia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Cuantificacion-EVIA.pdf); [Wikipedia: Quantification (ML)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantification_(machine_learning)); [QuaPy methods manual](https://gitea1.its.isti.cnr.it/moreo/QuaPy/raw/branch/devel/docs/source/manuals/methods.md). Some papers cite the adjusted method to Forman 2005 instead — [arXiv 2310.09210](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09210); [arXiv 2503.09395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.09395).
- Bella et al. (2010) Probabilistic Classify & Count (PCC) uses posterior probabilities instead of hard labels; PACC replaces the crisp CC count in the adjustment with the expected soft count — [QuaPy methods manual](https://gitea1.its.isti.cnr.it/moreo/QuaPy/raw/branch/devel/docs/source/manuals/methods.md); [QuaPy paper arXiv 2106.11057](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.11057); [mlquantify R PCC doc](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/mlquantify/html/PCC.html).
- González, Castaño, Chawla & del Coz (2017), "A Review on Quantification Learning", ACM Computing Surveys 50(5), art. 74, 1–40, DOI 10.1145/3117807 — unified review / tutorial of quantification methods (class distribution estimation, prevalence estimation) — [DBLP record](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/pid/10/5454.html); [Univ. Oviedo repository](https://digibuo.uniovi.es/dspace/handle/10651/45313?show=full).
- Moreo & Sebastiani (2021), "Re-Assessing the Classify and Count Quantification Method": earlier comparisons used poorly optimised CC; properly tuned CC is a stronger baseline — [Academia record](https://www.academia.edu/103774107/Re_Assessing_the_Classify_and_Count_Quantification_Method).
- Precision of a quantifier is driven by classifier discriminatory power, size of the labelled training set, and size of the unlabelled set being quantified (Bayesian quantifier preprint, 2025) — [arXiv 2507.06061](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2507.06061).
- Calibration and quantification are formally interconnected under dataset shift — [arXiv 2505.11380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11380); an EM prior-shift adjuster (Saerens et al. 2002) can optionally apply temperature/vector scaling before iterating — [prior-shift EM implementation](https://lab.atomdrift.org/file/2c87c6d3a2b377b30fb595372a33f34c7d7fd46cce3a3ec557219014a1c9f6ba) (secondary).
- Ordinal quantification methods exist for ordered classes (regularisation-based) — [arXiv 2310.09210](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09210).
- Software: QuaPy (Python) implements CC, ACC, PCC, PACC, EM and more — [arXiv 2106.11057](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.11057).
- [AG] No agricultural application of quantification learning found; the nearest are maturity-as-distribution papers — [arXiv 2510.24885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24885v1); [arXiv 2604.26084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26084).

### Inferences
- The right order of operations for the robot is: track → fuse per-track probabilities (one decision/one posterior per fruit) → count per class (CC) → apply ACC/PACC using the per-track confusion matrix estimated on validation **tracks** (not frames). Using the frame-level confusion matrix would be wrong because the fused per-track classifier has a different (better) confusion matrix.
- PACC pairs naturally with mean-probability fusion: summing the fused per-track posterior vectors gives the soft count directly; the multiclass ACC solves C·p = p̂_CC for the 5×5 confusion matrix C (needs enough validation tracks per class for a well-conditioned C).
- Because quantification corrects aggregate bias while fusion corrects individual labels, the thesis can show both effects separately: fusion reduces per-fruit error, ACC/PACC removes the residual systematic bias in the 5-class distribution (and note that González et al.'s precision drivers imply bias correction is only as good as the validation-set confusion estimate).

### Gaps
- No example found of quantification applied after multi-object tracking in any domain (searched agriculture-specific); the thesis would be, as far as these searches show, the first to combine them for ripeness.
- Full text of Forman 2008 and González 2017 not accessed; formulas come from a Spanish tutorial deck and QuaPy documentation.

## KQ6. Calibration of softmax probabilities (temperature scaling) as a prerequisite for probability averaging

### Takeaway
Guo et al. (ICML 2017) show modern deep nets are over-confident and that temperature scaling (one scalar T fitted on a held-out set, dividing logits) is the most effective simple fix; follow-up work shows that **averaging already-calibrated members** ("pre-combination" calibration) is the appropriate regime for probability fusion, while averaging uncalibrated over-confident members pushes the ensemble toward under-confidence that then needs a post-hoc temperature. Exact ECE tables from Guo et al. could not be verified here (only MCE for ResNet-110/CIFAR-100: 35.5 % → 4.74 %).

### Cited Findings
- Guo, Pleiss, Sun & Weinberger, "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks", ICML 2017 (PMLR v70): modern networks, unlike those of a decade ago, are poorly calibrated; **temperature scaling, a single-parameter variant of Platt scaling, "is surprisingly effective" / often the most effective** calibration method; ECE (binned |accuracy − confidence| weighted average) is the primary metric; Figure 1 contrasts a 5-layer LeNet with a 110-layer ResNet on CIFAR-100 — [PMLR v70 guo17a](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html); [arXiv 1706.04599](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599v1); [ICML talk](https://icml.cc/virtual/2017/talk/1363).
- Guo et al. supplement: for **CIFAR-100 / ResNet-110, MCE drops from 35.5 % uncalibrated to 4.74 % with temperature scaling** (supplement table reports MCE, test error, NLL — not ECE) (primary excerpt) — [arXiv HTML 1706.04599](https://arxiv.org/html/1706.04599).
- Third-party replication (Neural Clamping, 2022): ResNet-110 on CIFAR-100, accuracy 74.15 %, **ECE 0.1074 uncalibrated → 0.0172 with temperature scaling** (different setup; do not cite as Guo's number) — [arXiv 2209.11604](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.11604).
- Temperature scaling preserves the argmax (monotone rescaling of logits), so it changes probabilities but not per-frame accuracy — stated in the course slides summarising Guo et al. — [FIU lecture slides](https://users.cs.fiu.edu/~sjha/class2023/Lecture8/Slides/2017TemperatureScaling.pdf).
- "Should Ensemble Members Be Calibrated?" (2021): defines **pre-combination** calibration (temperature-scale each member before averaging) vs post-combination; notes histogram binning is unsuitable pre-combination because its outputs may not form a valid distribution — [arXiv 2101.05397](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.05397).
- Deep-ensemble ENSO paper: averaging can push predictions toward **under-confidence**, hurting ECE unless the members are over-confident; proposes temperature scaling after averaging, citing Rahaman & Thiery (2021) for applying it to ensemble probabilities — [arXiv 2512.17153](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17153).
- scikit-learn `CalibratedClassifierCV(method='temperature')` fits one temperature per CV fold and averages the calibrated probabilities — [scikit-learn calibration.py mirror](https://gitverse.ru/githubmirror/scikit-learn/content/main/sklearn/calibration.py).
- Ensemble temperature scaling variants exist (GETS for GNNs) — [arXiv 2410.09570](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.09570).
- Calibration also matters for quantification: calibrated posteriors are what PCC/PACC and EM prior-shift methods consume — [arXiv 2505.11380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11380).
- Albumentations: averaging post-softmax probabilities down-weights over-confident wrong predictions relative to logit averaging — [Albumentations TTA guide](https://albumentations.ai/docs/4-advanced-guides/test-time-augmentation/).

### Inferences
- For the two-stage pipeline, fit T on a held-out set of **crops** (per-frame) and apply it before track averaging — this is the pre-combination regime of arXiv 2101.05397. For the single-stage YOLO, the per-box class scores are not a softmax over the 5 classes (YOLOv8-style heads use per-class sigmoids), so they should first be renormalised/calibrated to a proper 5-class distribution (e.g., softmax over class logits with a fitted T, or vector scaling) before mean-probability or expected-stage fusion.
- Because the fused per-track posterior will tend toward under-confidence after averaging (arXiv 2512.17153), a second temperature can be fitted at the track level if calibrated per-fruit confidences are needed (e.g., for abstaining on ambiguous fruits).
- Calibration is irrelevant for hard majority vote and for argmax-of-mean only insofar as T is shared across frames (a common T does not change the argmax of the mean when all frames share it? — it *can*, since mean of softmax(z/T) is not argmax-invariant in T); it matters most for log-sum, expected-stage and PACC, where the probability magnitudes are used.

### Gaps
- Guo et al.'s main ECE table (e.g., ResNet-110 CIFAR-100 ECE before/after) could not be read; only MCE values were visible. Verify from the PDF.
- No study found that measures the effect of temperature scaling on *accuracy of probability-averaged track decisions* (as opposed to ECE of the ensemble); evidence is theoretical (pre- vs post-combination) rather than numerical.

## Consolidated recommendation for the thesis (synthesis across KQs — inferences)

1. **Fusion rule**: visibility-weighted mean of temperature-scaled per-frame 5-class probabilities, weight w_t = f(box area or visible fraction, detection confidence); decision = argmax of the weighted mean for categorical counts and E[stage] for a continuous ripeness index. Report hard majority vote, max-confidence, and log-sum (naive Bayes) as ablations; expect mean ≥ vote ≥ max and log-sum to be over-confident under correlated frames (Kittler 1998; Kittler & Alkoot 2003; Seq-NMS avg vs max; TTA diversity theory).
2. **When to decide**: after N_min confirmed frames (ByteTrack `minimum_consecutive_frames`), with N_min chosen from a validation curve of counting error and per-track accuracy vs N; by analogy with multi-view recognition (3 views ≈ 80 views; median of 3 most-visible frames in apple counting), expect saturation within a handful of frames. Tracks shorter than N_min are not counted and their number is reported.
3. **Count bias**: after per-track decisions, apply ACC/PACC with a 5×5 confusion matrix estimated on validation *tracks*; this is unprecedented in the fruit literature per these searches.
4. **Evidence to cite for "fusion helps under occlusion"**: apple-conveyor track aggregation (2026), avocado 6-view +7.7 pts, Hwang et al. CVPR 2022 (+4.1 TrackAP50, motivated by occlusion/blur), Seq-NMS (+6.5 mAP), Hofmann 2011 multi-camera under occlusion — flagging the last three as non-agricultural.
